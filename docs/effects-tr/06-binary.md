# §6 The binary relation: fundamental property and soundness

[← §5 The unary relation](05-unary.md) · [index](README.md) · next: [§7 The laws](07-laws.md)

The binary relation is phase B's (`~/repos/borrow-core-effmodel-rel`, branch `effmodel-rel`,
`experiments/EffModelRel/EffModelRel/Rel.lean`). Phase B has proved its Fundamental Property,
its adequacy and its soundness for denotational contextual equivalence in Lean; this section
transcribes the definitions, cites the Lean proof for each lemma, and adds what this calculus
needs beyond phase B:

- the three small formers phase B does not have (L6.4);
- **agreement** of the machine with the trees on moded programs (T6.6, new);
- **soundness for the machine's contextual equivalence** (T6.7, new).

The relation is defined on the trees of §3.2, over the instance supply of D4.7, with no
resource. It does not read modes, grades or rows. So every law it proves holds for every
intrinsically typed term, moded or not, and the moded calculus inherits it (T6.7).

**No step index.** `TreeRel` is an inductive relation on finite trees, the value relation is by
recursion on types, and the one recursive type is interpreted by a least fixed point. Phase B's
`Rel.lean` header: "Trees are finite (the language has no unbounded recursion), so `TreeRel` is
an inductive relation".

## 6.1 Definitions

**Definition D6.1 (related actions and destructors).** [PB `AR`, `DtorR`, `DR`.] At a world `w`,
`out n ~ out n`; `acq ℓ r ~ acq ℓ r'`, `use ℓ r ~ use ℓ r'`, `free ℓ r ~ free ℓ r'` when
`w.r(r, r')`; `new c s ~ new c' s`, `rd c G ~ rd c' G`, `wr c s ~ wr c' s`, `kill c G ~ kill c' G`
when `w.r(c, c')`; and, added here, `punch n ~ punch n`. Destructor lists are related pointwise.

**Definition D6.2 (related trees).** [PB `TreeRel`.] For a relation `R` on leaves indexed by
worlds, `TreeRel_R(w, t, t')` is the inductive relation generated by:

| rule | conclusion | premises |
|---|---|---|
| `ret` | `ret x ~ ret x'` | `w.noPend`, `R(w, x, x')` |
| `op` | `op i E o v k d ~ op i' E o v k' d'` | `w.noPend`, `w.r(i, i')`, `d ~ d'`, `∀ y. k y ~ k' y` |
| `ab` | `ab i E a v d ~ ab i' E a v d'` | `w.noPend`, `w.r(i, i')`, `d ~ d'` |
| `abE` | `ab i E a v (d ++ f) ~ Tree.dtors d' (ab i' E a v f')` | `w.noPend`, `w.r(i, i')`, `d ~ d'`, `f ~ f'` |
| `act` | `act α k ~ act α' k'` | `α ~ α'`, `∀ y. k y ~ k' y` |
| `nu` | `nu k ~ nu k'` | `∀ i i' free. k i ~ k' i'` at `w.both(i, i')` |
| `nuL`, `nuR` | `nu k ~ t'`, `t ~ nu k'` | the new name pending, at `w.pendL(i)` / `w.pendR(i')` |
| `gL`, `gR` | `nu k ~ t'`, `t ~ nu k'` | the new name garbage, at `w.garbL(i)` / `w.garbR(i')` |
| `mL`, `mR` | `nu k ~ t'`, `t ~ nu k'` | the new name matched with the other side's pending one |

`abE` is the destructor form of algebraicity built into the relation: an abort that carries
destructors is related to running them first. Every name must be matched before anything other
than allocation happens to it: the leaves, operations and aborts need `w.noPend`.

**Definition D6.3 (streams).** [PB `StrmLeaf`, `StrmClosed`, `StrmRel`.] `StrmRel_{RA}(w, s, s')`
is the least relation closed under one unfolding: `mk p d ~ mk p' d'` when
`TreeRel_{StrmLeaf(StrmRel, RA)}(w, p, p')` and `TreeRel_⊤(w, d, d')`. It is defined
impredicatively, as the intersection of all closed relations, and its unfolding lemma
`StrmRel.unf` and introduction `StrmRel.intro` are proved.

**Definition D6.4 (value and computation relations).** [PB `VR`, `CRU`.] By recursion on types:

    VR(G, w, a, a')          ≜ a = a'                    (also at tkt)
    VR(A ⊗ B, w, a, a')      ≜ VR(A, w, a.1, a'.1) ∧ VR(B, w, a.2, a'.2)
    VR(A ⊕ B, w, x, x')      ≜ componentwise; False on different injections
    VR(U_m^ρ C, w, c, c')     ≜ CRU(C, gcP(w), c, c')
    VR(cap_E S, w, c, c')     ≜ w.r(c.1, c'.1) ∧ ∀ w' ≥ gcP(w). ∀ o a s. TreeRel_=(w', c.2 o a s, c'.2 o a s)
    VR(strm A, w, s, s')      ≜ StrmRel_{VR(A)}(gcP(w), s, s')
    CRU(F A, w, t, t')        ≜ TreeRel_{VR(A)}(w, t, t')
    CRU(A ⇒ C, w, f, f')      ≜ ∀ w' ≥ w. ∀ a a'. VR(A, w', a, a') ⇒ CRU(C, w', f a, f' a')
    CRU(C & D, w, p, p')      ≜ CRU(C, w, p.1, p'.1) ∧ CRU(D, w, p.2, p'.2)
    CRU(Π(x:E). C, w, f, f')  ≜ ∀ w' ≥ w. ∀ i i'. w'.r(i, i') ⇒ CRU(C, w', f i, f' i')
    CRU(∀(r ≤ b). C, …)       ≜ CRU(C, …)                (rows are erased)

Values are related at `gcP(w)`, pending names read as garbage, so a value's relation does not
change while the two sides line up their allocations.

**Definition D6.5 (environments and the logical relation).** [PB `IR`, `ER`, `LR`.]
`IR(w, η, η')` ≜ every instance variable names matched instances (for a `Res` instance, the same
label and matched resource names); `ER(w, ρ, ρ')` ≜ every value variable is related at its type;

    LR(M, N)  ≜  ∀ w. w.noPend ⇒ ∀ η η' ρ ρ'. IR(w, η, η') ⇒ ER(w, ρ, ρ') ⇒ CRU(C, w, ⟦M⟧ η ρ, ⟦N⟧ η' ρ')

## 6.2 Lemmas

Each is proved in phase B's Lean; the proof sketch says what the proof is, for the
transcription.

**Lemma L6.1 (monotonicity).** [PB `TreeRel.mono`, `TreeRel.mapR`, `StrmRel.mono`, `VR.mono`,
`CRU.mono`, `IR.mono`, `ER.mono`.] `TreeRel` is monotone in `≤` when its leaf relation is; `VR` is
monotone in `⊑`; `CRU` in `≤`.

*Proof.* Induction on `TreeRel`, using L4.3's monotonicity of each world update; `VR` and `CRU`
by mutual recursion on types. ∎

**Lemma L6.2 (every computation type's model preserves the relation).** [PB `opA_rel`,
`abA_rel`, `actA_rel`, `nuA_both`, `nuA_L`, `nuA_R`, `nuA_gL`, `nuA_gR`, `nuA_mL`, `nuA_mR`,
`dtorsA_rel`, `dtors_rel`, `abA_dtors_rel`.]

*Proof.* By recursion on `C`: at `F` the `TreeRel` constructors; at `⇒`, `&`, `Π` pointwise from
the induction hypothesis. `abA_dtors_rel` is `abE` lifted to every type, using that destructors
commute with the model operations (`dtorsA_F`, `dtorsA_append`, `dtorsA_arr`, `dtorsA_wth`,
`dtorsA_pi`). ∎

**Lemma L6.3 (the folds preserve the relation).** [PB `bindC_rel`, `Tree.bind_rel`, `hnd_rel`,
`addFin_rel`, `runL_rel`.] For related trees at `w`:

1. sequencing into any `C` with continuations related at every `w' ⊒ w` with nothing pending;
2. a deep handler of matched instances `w.r(i, i')` with clauses related at every later world;
3. a `using` frame with matched resource names;
4. a lend of matched cells and instances, with co-operations related at every later world;

each gives related results.

*Proof.* Each by induction on `TreeRel`. In `hnd_rel` the decisive step is that a handler
catches an operation on the left exactly when it catches the related one on the right:
`w.r_iff` (the matching is a bijection). In `runL_rel`, a co-operation's failure branch runs the
client's destructors, related by `dtors_rel`. ∎

**Lemma L6.4 (this calculus's additions).** The relation is preserved by:

1. `lend` answering `(cap, z)`: its denotation is phase B's followed by `Tree.bind` with
   `λz. ret ((c, co), z)` (D3.13), so L6.3.1 at `Tree.bind_rel`, with the capability related by
   `w.r(c, c')` and `CoOK`-free co-operation relatedness (PB `cap_rel`, `coSem_rel`);
2. `using x` binding a `Res` instance and `use` as `actA (use ℓ r)`: `IR` gives `w.r(r, r')` and the
   same label, so `actA_rel` with `AR.use`;
3. `punch`: `actA_rel` with `punch n ~ punch n`; `tkt` is related by equality;
4. row abstraction and application: erased by the denotation, so the identity.

*Proof.* Each is one application of the cited lemma. (1) is `Tree.bind_rel` after phase B's
`runL_rel` proof of `Cmp.fp`'s `lend` case, and the pair's components are related: the
capability at `VR(cap)` by `cap_rel`'s argument, the sum by phase B's own case. ∎

**Theorem T6.5 (Fundamental Property, binary).** For every intrinsically typed term,
`LR(M, M)`; for every value, related to itself.

*Proof.* [PB `Val.fp`, `Cmp.fp`, `fundamental`.] By structural recursion on the term, one case
per former, each a lemma above: `bind` by L6.3.1; `handle` by `nuA_both` (both sides allocate
and match) then L6.3.2; `handleAt` by L6.3.2 at `IR`; `nu` by `nuA_both`; `ieq` by `w.r_iff`;
`newcap` by `TreeRel.nu` and `cap_rel`; `lend` by L6.3.4 (and L6.4.1); `using` by L6.3.3;
`unfold`/`fold` by `StrmRel.unf`/`StrmRel.intro`; `bfix` by induction on `n`; the additions by
L6.4. ∎

**Lemma L6.5 (adequacy of the relation).** [PB `Inv`, `AR.exec_rel`, `DR.unwind_rel`,
`adequacy`.] If `TreeRel_R(w, t, t')`, the leaf relation renders related leaves equally, and the
two tree-worlds agree with `w` (`Inv`: the allocated names are the world's, matched cells hold
equal contents, other cells are empty, the traces are equal), then `obs(t) = obs(t')`.

*Proof.* Induction on `TreeRel`; each allocation case extends `Inv` (`Inv.link'`, `Inv.setL'`,
`Inv.setR'`); `abE` uses `obs_dtors_ab`. ∎

**Corollary C6.6 (soundness, denotational).** [PB `soundness`.] `LR(M, N) ⇒ CtxEq_den(M, N)`.

*Proof.* The closures of related terms are related (`close_rel`, from `curryI_rel` and
`curryE_rel`); the context is related to itself (T6.5); L6.5 at `W.init`, `Inv.init`. ∎

## 6.3 Agreement of the machine with the trees

Phase B's equivalence observes trees. The machine (§3.1) is the operational semantics. They
differ in four ways: the machine updates a lent cell in place where trees thread its state and
write it back (`rd`/`wr`); the machine keeps one-shot continuations in heap cells; the machine
allocates names for continuations, which trees do not; and the machine gets stuck where trees,
being functions, would run a continuation twice. On moded programs none of these is
observable. This is new; phase B has no machine.

**Definition D6.6 (decoding).** For a heap `μ`, decode machine values, stacks and
configurations into the semantic domain:

    d(clo(γ, M))        = ⟦M⟧ (d ∘ γ)
    d(cap(ℓ, co))       = (ℓ, ⟦co⟧)
    d(κ¹(ℓ_κ, w))       = mkK_w(λy. D(S_κ)(ret y), Tree.dtors(dtors S_κ)(ret ()))   if μ(ℓ_κ) = seg(S_κ)
                        = an arbitrary element                                       otherwise
    d(κ*(S_κ, w))       = mkK_w(λy. D(S_κ)(ret y), Tree.dtors(dtors S_κ)(ret ()))
    (and componentwise on data)

    D([])                          = id
    D(bind(γ, x.N) :: S)(t)        = D(S)(bindC t (λv. ⟦N⟧ (d ∘ γ[x ↦ v])))
    D(arg v̂ :: S)(c)               = D(S)(c (d v̂))        (prjᵢ, iarg j alike)
    D(hnd(j, γ, H) :: S)(t)        = D(S)(hnd(j, E, ⟦H⟧(d ∘ γ))(t))
    D(run(j, ℓ, co) :: S)(t)       = D(S)(Tree.bind (runL(j, E, ℓ, S, ⟦co⟧, s_ℓ)(t)) (λz. ret ((ℓ, ⟦co⟧), z)))
                                      where μ(ℓ) = cell(s_ℓ)
    D(fin(j, ℓ_r, ℓ) :: S)(t)      = D(S)(addFin(ℓ, ℓ_r)(t))
    D(coret(j, ℓ) :: S)(t)         = Tree.bind t (coK …)   (the rest of runL's coStep, D3.12)

`T(⟨μ, n, tr ; S ; c⟩) ≜ D(S)(⟦c⟧)`, with `⟦ev(M, γ)⟧ = ⟦M⟧(d ∘ γ)` and `⟦rt v̂⟧ = ret (d v̂)`, and the
tree-world `Wd(⟨μ, n, tr ; …⟩) ≜ (n, μ restricted to cell(_) cells, tr)`.

**Lemma L6.7 (decoding facts).**

1. `D(S ++ S')(t) = D(S')(D(S)(t))`.
2. If `S` handles no name `j`, then `D(S)` maps `op j E o v k d` to `op j E o v (D(S) ∘ k) (d ++ δ(S))`,
   and `ab j E a v d` to `ab j E a v (d ++ δ(S))`, where `δ(S)` lists `free ℓ ℓ_r` for each
   `fin` frame and `wb ℓ s_ℓ` for each `run` frame of `S`, innermost first.
3. `obs` is invariant under injective renaming of names (in the tree and the world together).

*Proof.* (1) By induction on `S`. (2) By induction on `S`: `bindC`, `hnd` of another name and the
model operations pass an operation through (`Tree.bindC`, `Tree.hnd`'s non-matching branch,
`CTy.opA`); `addFin` appends `free`; `runL` of another name appends `wb`. (3) `Tree.obs` reads
names only to compare them and to look up the store. ∎

**Theorem T6.6 (agreement).** For every closed moded program `·;·;· ⊢ P : F G ! ∅`,
`Obs_op(P) = obs(⟦P⟧)`.

*Proof.* By T5.2 the run of `P` is finite and ends at `done ĝ` with an empty heap, never stuck,
never unhandled. Show, for each step `cfg → cfg'` of that run,

    obs from (T(cfg), Wd(cfg))  =  obs from (T(cfg'), Wd(cfg'))     (up to renaming, L6.7.3)

where "obs from" is `Tree.obs` started at the given world, its trace appended to the world's.
At the start `T = ⟦P⟧`, `Wd = Wd.init`; at `done ĝ`, `T = ret ĝ`. By induction on the run,
`obs(⟦P⟧) = (ret ĝ, tr) = Obs_op(P)`. The cases, by the row of D3.6:

- **Pure steps** (`to`, `λ`, application, `prj`, records, `split`, `case`, `if`, `prim`, `force` of a
  closure, `bfix`, instance and row abstraction): `T(cfg) = T(cfg')` by the defining equations of
  `⟦·⟧` (e.g. `bindC (ret v) f = f v`; `⟦M V⟧ = ⟦M⟧ (⟦V⟧)`; `iter`).
- **`handle`, `ν`, `newcap`, `using`, `lend`**: the machine takes `n` from its counter; the tree's
  `nu` takes the world's next name, which is `n` up to the renaming that also covers the names
  the machine gave continuations. `newcap`: `act (new …)` emits `mk` and stores the state, as the
  machine does. `using`: `act (acq ℓ r)` emits `acq ℓ`. `lend`: `act (rd c S)` reads the live cell
  and emits nothing; the machine does not read. Then the frame's decoding is the tree's fold.
- **Emitting steps** (`emit`, `punch`, `use`, `getcap`): the same event; `getcap`'s `kill` and the
  machine's removal agree on the store.
- **An operation on a handler** (`f = hnd(j, γ_h, H)`, `S = S₁ ++ f :: S₂`): by L6.7.1–2,
  `T(cfg) = D(S₂)(hnd_j(op j o v (λy. D(S₁)(⟦M⟧ y)) δ(S₁)))`, which by `catchOp_self`
  [PB] is `D(S₂)(hop o v (λy. hnd_j(D(S₁)(⟦M⟧ y))) δ(S₁))`. The machine enters `N_o` with
  `k ↦ κ`, and `d(κ) = mkK(λy. D(S_κ)(ret y), Tree.dtors(dtors S_κ)(ret ()))` with
  `D(S_κ)(ret y) = hnd_j(D(S₁)(⟦M⟧ y))` (L6.7.1). The cancel components agree because
  `δ(S₁) = dtors(S₁)` here: `S₁` has no `run` frame when `W ∈ q_o`, by the [lend] premise (a
  lend body performs only `lin` operations of outer instances), so `δ` contributes only
  `free`s. (When `W ∉ q_o` there is no cancel component.)
- **An abortive operation**: as above with `catchAb_self`: the tree runs `dtorsA δ(S₁)` then
  `hab`; the machine runs `dtors(S₁)` then `N_a`. Again `S₁` has no `run` frame: an abortive
  instance in a lend body's row has `cancel` in its bound, which [lend] refuses.
- **An operation on a runner**: the tree's `coStep` runs `⟦co⟧ o v s_ℓ` and binds `coK`; the
  machine runs `N_o` above `coret`. Their decodings agree by `D(coret …)`. The machine's write of
  the new state into `ℓ` is the tree's threading of `s'` into `runL`: the run frame decodes with
  the current `μ(ℓ)`. This is where in-place update and state threading meet, and it needs that
  no other step reads or writes `ℓ` while the run frame is live: by L5.15 the cell is owned by
  the frame, so in a run of a moded program (T5.2) only the frame's steps touch it.
- **A co-operation's failure**: `coK`'s failure branch runs `Tree.dtors d` then writes the state
  and answers `inr e`; the machine runs `dtors(S₁)` up to the run frame and answers
  `(cap, inr e)`. `S₁` holds no `run` frame (as above, the runner's fallible operation has
  `cancel`).
- **A return through `run`**: `runL`'s `ret` case writes the state (`wr`, no event, the cell is
  live) and answers `inl x`; the machine answers `(cap, inl v̂)`.
- **A return through `fin`, a cancel**: `addFin`'s `free`; `Tree.dtors`'s `free`s; the machine's
  removals with the same events.
- **Forcing a continuation** (`resume`): `T(cfg) = D(S)((d κ).1 w) = D(S)(D(S_κ)(ret w)) =
  D(S_κ ++ S)(ret w) = T(cfg')` (L6.7.1). For `κ¹`, `μ(ℓ_κ) = seg(S_κ)` holds because the run is
  not stuck (T5.2), so `d(κ¹)` is the first clause of D6.6. The arbitrary clause of D6.6 is never
  reached by `obs`: `obs` follows the same path as the machine, which never forces a removed cell. ∎

**Status.** T6.6 is proved here at the level of its cases. The decoding of stacks is the one
place this document introduces a definition with no counterpart in phase A or B. Its
mechanisation needs a typed decoding (`D` is typed by the frames' types), which is routine
bookkeeping but not small.

**Theorem T6.7 (soundness for contextual equivalence).** For moded `M, N` with
`Ψ; Θ; Γ ⊢ M, N : C ! ρ`: `LR(M, N) ⇒ CtxEq_op(M, N)`.

*Proof.* Let `P` be a moded context. `P[hole(M)/h]` is a closed moded program (substitution of a
closed value, L2.3 and the [thunk] rule at no captures). By T6.6,
`Obs_op(P[hole(M)/h]) = obs(⟦P[hole(M)/h]⟧)`. By the substitution property of the denotation
(phase B's `Cmp.den_ren` at a renaming, and by direct induction at a closed value),
`⟦P[hole(M)/h]⟧ = ⟦P⟧(h ↦ ⟦hole(M)⟧) = ⟦P⟧(h ↦ close ⟦M⟧)` (D3.15: `close` is the currying of
`⟦M⟧` over its instances and values, which is the denotation of `Λx⃗. λy⃗. M`). The erasure of
`P` is a context of D3.15. By C6.6, `obs(⟦P⟧(close ⟦M⟧)) = obs(⟦P⟧(close ⟦N⟧))`, and back through
T6.6 for `N`. ∎

**Corollary C6.8.** `LR(M, N)`, `CtxEq_den(M, N)` and `CtxEq_op(M, N)` hold of every law of §7
whose two sides are moded; and each law's `CtxEq_den` holds for all intrinsically typed instances,
moded or not.

## 6.4 Where this section differs from phase B, and why

| point | phase B | here | effect on phase B's proofs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lend`'s answer | `F (A ⊕ int)` | `F (cap ⊗ (A ⊕ int))` | one `Tree.bind_rel` (L6.4.1) |
| `using`'s handle | a value `(ℓ, r) : res` | an instance of `Res` | `IR` in place of `ER` for it (L6.4.2) |
| `punch`, `tkt` | absent | an action and a ground-like value | L6.4.3 |
| rows, row abstraction | absent | erased | identity (L6.4.4) |
| co-operation failure | at every operation | only at fallible ones (`E_o = 0` otherwise) | none: a special case |
| modes and grades | none (`Moded.lean` planned) | the moded predicate of §2 | none: the relation ignores them |
| contextual equivalence | over trees | over trees and over the machine | T6.6, T6.7 added |

Nothing phase B has proved is contradicted. Every law of §7 that phase B states is stated there in
the same form.
